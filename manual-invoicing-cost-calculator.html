Where the time goes

A “simple” invoice is rarely one step. Someone copies the customer's details, fills in the lines, checks the sums, makes a PDF, writes the email, records it in a spreadsheet and, later, chases the payment. Each mistake adds a correction, an apology and a payment that waits. The hidden cost of manual invoicing breaks this down, and from manual to automated shows what changes when the steps are automated, and what still needs a person.

Common questions

How much time does manual invoicing really take?
It depends on your process. Time one ordinary invoice from start to finish: finding the customer's details, keying the lines, checking the totals, making the PDF, sending it and recording it. Add the time spent chasing payment. Most owners find the real figure is higher than they guessed.
Why does the calculator count errors separately?
Because a wrong invoice costs more than the time to key it. Someone has to notice, issue a correction, reply to the customer and reconcile the accounts, and the payment waits meanwhile. Enter how often that happens and how long a fix takes.
What doesn't the calculator count?
Payments that arrive later because invoices go out late, revenue lost to pricing mistakes, and what your staff could have done with the time instead. These can matter more than the hours, but they vary too much between businesses to estimate here.
Does LHDN e-invoicing change this?
Yes, it adds a step. Each e-invoice has to be sent to LHDN's MyInvois system, through the MyInvois Portal or an API, and validated before you share it with the buyer. If you key invoices by hand today, entering them in the MyInvois Portal can mean keying them a second time.
Will automating invoicing save me this whole amount?
No. Someone still has to review and approve invoices and deal with exceptions, and automation has its own cost. The figure here is what the manual process costs today, which is the number to weigh any change against.
