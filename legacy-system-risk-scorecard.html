    4. Your backups are untested.

    How the score works

    Each question has a weight, and the weights add up to 100. “Yes” scores the full weight, “Not sure” half of it and “No” nothing. Scores under 20 are low, 20 to 44 moderate, 45 to 69 high and 70 or more critical. The heaviest weights go to the risks that leave you with no way back on a bad day.

    Common questions

    Is this scorecard an audit of my system?
    No. It is a rule of thumb built from the problems we see most often when we take over legacy systems. It can't see your code, your server or your backups, so a low score doesn't prove the system is safe and a high one doesn't mean it is about to fail. Use it to decide what to check first.
    Why does “Not sure” count against me?
    Because not knowing is itself a finding. If nobody can say when a backup was last restored or which server version the system runs on, nobody is looking after it. “Not sure” scores half the weight of “Yes”, and finding out is the first step.
    What should I fix first?
    Usually access, code and data: make sure the business holds every login, a complete copy of the source code and a backup it has actually restored. Those are what you need on the day something goes wrong. The scorecard lists your top three in order of weight.
    The system works fine. Why should I worry?
    Working fine and about to fail often look the same from outside. Systems send warning signs for months before they fail, and fixing them on your own schedule is far cheaper than fixing an outage. Read the warning signs.
